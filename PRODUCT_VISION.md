# Rerack — Product Vision

## The problem

Lots of people want to start lifting but stay away from gyms. Beginners mostly worry about practical, learnable things: not knowing how the machines work, not knowing the etiquette, feeling watched, and not knowing where to start. Gym-industry surveys back this up, but they come from gyms themselves, so treat their numbers as directional. In a PureGym survey of 800+ members, the most common worry was not knowing how to use the machines (60%). Gym Group/OnePoll research found that "not knowing where to start" put off nearly a quarter of non-gym-goers.

These are knowledge gaps, and knowledge gaps can be taught before someone ever walks through the door.

## Who it's for

College students and young adults with little or no gym experience, plus any other beginner. They're comfortable with phone games and learning apps, put off by bodybuilding culture, and short on time.

## Positioning

> **Learn the gym before you walk in.**

Rerack is a short daily learning game, not a workout tracker, coach, or program generator. It promises familiarity and confidence through knowledge. It doesn't promise a body transformation.

## Product principles

1. **One thing at a time.** Each screen asks one question. Each level takes about three minutes.
2. **Mistakes teach.** Every wrong answer gets a specific explanation, and the question comes back once at the end of the level. There are no lives or hearts, and you can't fail out.
3. **Rewards track real progress.** XP comes from clearing levels for the first time, a one-time clean-sheet bonus, and one tune-up per day. Replays are for practice and can't farm XP.
4. **Gentle return loop.** A small daily goal (1–3 sessions) and a weekly calendar. Missing a day costs nothing, and the app never shames a learner.
5. **Never gamify risk.** No rewards for heavier weights, pushing through pain or training volume. Physical exercise is never needed to keep progress.
6. **Honest about itself.** Unwritten content is shown as "Being written". Local-only data is described as local-only.

## Concepts considered

| Direction | Summary | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Floor Map** | The learning path runs across a stylised gym floor plan, and each unit is a zone (Front Door → Body Basics → Equipment Floor…). Levels are weight-plate "stations" connected by floor tape. | **Chosen.** The map *is* the subject: moving through it mirrors moving through a real gym. It scales cleanly (one zone per unit), works vertically on phones, and gives the brand a clear visual system (wayfinding signage + bumper-plate colours). |
| B. Coach Chat | A guide character walks you through scenarios as a branching conversation. | Rejected. It looks like a generic AI chatbot, it's hard to make interactions varied, and it's slow to play. |
| C. Gym Pass Card Collection | You collect equipment "cards" and build decks into workouts. | Rejected for v1. Collection mechanics pull attention toward completion rather than understanding, and building workouts needs content (Units 4–5) that should be expert-reviewed first. Some of the ideas could come back later as a reference library. |

## Core loop

Open → see the next station glowing on the floor map → play a 3-minute level (6–8 single challenges with instant feedback) → missed items return once → results: XP, first-try score, the level's three key takeaways, any new badge → next station unlocks with a small animation → a tune-up later revisits what you missed.

## What success looks like (to measure later)

- Learners finish Unit 1 and report higher confidence about a first visit (a pre/post self-rating, as an opt-in survey).
- Return rate over the first week, measured *without* pressure mechanics.
- Tune-ups reduce repeat misses on the same concept.

None of these are measured in this build. It has no analytics.

## Not in scope (yet)

Accounts, cloud sync, social features, workout logging, personalised programming, paid features.
